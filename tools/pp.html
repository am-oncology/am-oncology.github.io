What "practising privileges" means

A licence a private hospital grants a consultant to treat patients using its facilities, without becoming an employee. You remain a self-employed independent contractor, but work under the hospital's clinical governance, policies and CQC registration.

04 — Getting paid

Billing, coding & fees

How money actually moves, which insurers set the pace, and what your procedures are worth. The fee schedule and estimator below use the CCSD codes and rates from your notes.

The billing workflow

Insured patients follow a fixed path; self-pay is direct.

Billing can be outsourced to a medical billing bureau instead of your secretary/PA. Typical bureau fee: 3–6% of the amount billed. It's a legal requirement to invoice via Healthcode — you can't send invoices straight to a PMI.

The insurers (PMIs)

Market share shapes how much fee freedom you have.

BUPA and AXA enforce fee caps ("fee-assured"); shortfall billing to the patient isn't allowed. WPA is the most flexible on fees. Others in the market: Allianz, Benenden, Cigna, Freedom, Healix, Exeter.

How oncology coding works

CCSD

The Clinical Coding & Schedule Development system — the industry-standard coding used across UK private insurers.

Chemotherapy

Billed per cycle by regimen length. Only one supervision code per course, whatever the drug combination — and you can't add a separate follow-up consultation fee.

Radiotherapy

Two billable parts: planning (X6000-series, one-off, tiered simple/complex/stereotactic) and supervision (X0007–X0009, X6581, a block by total fractions). Delivery codes (X7000s) are hospital-only.

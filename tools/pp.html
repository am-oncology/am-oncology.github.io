NHS Transition & Job Planning

What you need to know before seeing your first private patient

⚠️ Your NHS contract is the starting point for everything. Request a copy from HR if you don't have one. Rules differ meaningfully between full-time consultant, part-time, and SAS contracts.
📄

Understanding Your Contract & Job Plan

A full-time NHS consultant contract comprises 10 Programmed Activities (PAs) per week, each representing 4 hours. Private practice must occur entirely outside these committed PAs.

(DCC)
PA 7–8Teaching & admin
(SPA)
PA 9–10Research & CPD
(SPA)
⏰ Private Work — Evenings, weekends, or negotiated PA reductions. You cannot do private work during an on-call shift, even if not called in.
ℹ️
Part-time contracts: Working fewer than 10 PAs does not automatically free the remaining time for private work without Trust notification. The same declaration rules apply.
ℹ️
SAS grades: Specialty doctors and associates have different contractual provisions. Check specifically for any clause restricting outside practice and consult your BMA rep if unclear.

The 5 Stages: From Considering to Billing

1

Review Your Contract & the Trust's Conflicts of Interest Policy

Request your full employment contract from NHS HR. Look specifically for:

  • Explicit clauses permitting or restricting private practice
  • Any private practice agreement that formed part of your appointment
  • The Trust's Management of Conflicts of Interest Policy (usually on the intranet)
  • The NHSE 2023 updated guidance on consultant private practice
⏱ Allow 1–2 weeks
📁 Request from NHS HR
🔎 Search Trust intranet for "conflicts of interest"
2

Informal Conversation with Your Clinical Director

Before formal paperwork, have a collegial conversation. Most CDs are supportive — many have done the same. Discuss:

  • Where you intend to work (which private hospital or clinic, which days)
  • Confirmation that there is no conflict with NHS waiting lists
  • Whether any change to your job plan is needed or desired
💬 Informal first
📅 Follow up in writing
📝 Keep a note of the conversation
3

Submit Your Declaration of Interests

Most Trusts require submission via their online portal within 28 days of commencing private work. Your declaration must cover:

  • Financial interest type: "Direct financial — paid for clinical services"
  • Details: Name of private facility, estimated income bracket
  • Management plan: How you will prevent conflict (e.g., no NHS-to-private migration of patients)
⚠️ Declarations must be renewed annually and updated within 28 days of any material change. Non-declaration is a conduct matter under the NHS Code of Conduct.
4

Formal Written Notification to the Trust

Send the email template (below) to your Clinical Director, cc-ing the Medical Director and your PA. This establishes a documented record satisfying GMC Good Medical Practice (2024) and your employer's policy requirements.

📋 Keep a copy in your own records

Begin Parallel Registration & Facility Onboarding

Once your Trust is notified, proceed with the Registration checklist. Private hospital credentialing (HCA, Nuffield, Spire) typically takes 6–8 weeks and is the most common bottleneck — start it immediately, in parallel with everything else.

Common Pitfalls

Using NHS resources Rooms, equipment, imaging systems, PACS, or support staff cannot be used for private patients — even incidentally or "after hours."
NHS → private patient migration Seeing a patient privately who is concurrently on your NHS list — without explicit agreement and a documented rationale — is a serious conduct issue.
Private work in NHS time Includes writing private reports, taking private referral calls, or responding to private administration queries during contracted NHS hours.
Claims-made indemnity If you ever stop paying premiums, historical claims are unprotected. Oncology claims can emerge 5–10 years later. Always use occurrence-based cover.

1. GMC Licence to Practise

Log into GMC Online and confirm your Licence to Practise is active. Check your revalidation date — if it falls within 6 months of your planned start, address revalidation first. Private hospitals independently verify your GMC status before granting practising privileges.

gmc-uk.org Included in annual GMC fee Immediate — just verify

2. Medical Indemnity — Occurrence-Based Only

This is the most commonly misunderstood requirement. For private practice in oncology you must have occurrence-based indemnity, not claims-made. The distinction is critical.

⚠️
Occurrence-based: You are protected for any incident that occurred while your policy was active — even if a claim is made years after you stop paying.
Claims-made: Only protects you if the claim is lodged while the policy is still active. If you stop paying, historical claims are unprotected.

In oncology, claims routinely emerge 5–10 years after treatment. Claims-made cover is therefore unsuitable.

Main providers: MDU, MPS, Howden, MDAM. Get quotes from at least two. Howden has been competitive for oncology in 2025–26.

MDU / MPS / Howden / MDAM ~£3,000–£8,000/yr (oncology) 1–2 weeks to arrange

3. ICO Registration — Data Controller

As soon as you process any patient data (including bookings and enquiries) you are a Data Controller under UK GDPR and must register with the ICO. This applies even if you use a third-party practice management system. Registration takes approximately 10 minutes online.

  • Visit ico.org.uk/registration → "Register for a data protection fee"
  • Select Tier 1 (small organisation / sole trader) — £40/year
  • You receive a Registration Reference Number — record it; hospitals will ask for it
  • You must publish a Privacy Notice on your website; the ICO provides a template
ico.org.uk £40/year Same-day registration

4. Enhanced DBS Certificate

Required for all patient-facing practitioners. There is no official statutory expiry, but most private hospitals require a certificate issued within the last 3 years. If yours is older, apply for a new one. Consider joining the DBS Update Service (£13/year) — it allows hospitals to check your certificate status in real time without re-application each time.

gov.uk/dbs-check £38 standard / ~£60–70 via umbrella body 2–4 weeks

5. CQC Registration (Conditional — most will not need this)

CQC registration is mandatory only if you provide regulated activities in premises that you control — e.g., your own consulting rooms or an independent clinic you operate. If you work exclusively as a sessional consultant at an established private hospital (HCA, Nuffield, Spire, UCLH Private), their CQC registration covers your practice. You do not need your own registration in this scenario.

ℹ️ Uncertain? Call the CQC helpline (03000 616161) to describe your arrangements. The answer is almost always "no separate registration needed" for standard sessional work. Only pursue CQC registration if you plan to operate independent premises.
cqc.org.uk £1,500+ fee + significant paperwork 3–6 months if required

6. PHIN Registration — Fee Transparency (Legal Requirement)

Since 2019 it has been a legal requirement for all consultants treating PMI patients to publish their fees via the Private Healthcare Information Network (PHIN) and submit outcome data for specified procedures. PMI companies check this independently — some will not process claims from unregistered consultants.

  • Register at phin.org.uk → "Register as a consultant"
  • Publish your consultation fee and any procedure fees
  • Submit outcome data annually for relevant PHIN-defined procedures
  • Ensure your fees are also visible on your website (hyperlink to PHIN profile is acceptable)
phin.org.uk Free 1 week to register

7. Healthcode ePractice Account

Healthcode is the electronic data interchange gateway used by virtually all PMI companies in the UK. Without an ePractice account you cannot submit electronic invoices to Bupa, AXA, Aviva, Cigna, or most others. Your Healthcode Billing Reference appears on every invoice.

  • Register at healthcode.co.uk → "Join Healthcode"
  • You'll need: GMC number, indemnity policy number, bank details, and ICO registration number
  • Most practice management systems (Semble, WriteUpp) integrate directly with Healthcode
  • Healthcode also runs compliance checks — most complete within 1–2 weeks
healthcode.co.uk ~£15/month 1–2 weeks

8. Private Hospital Credentialing (Start immediately — biggest bottleneck)

Each private hospital runs its own credentialing process, completely independent of your NHS post and GMC registration. Expect to provide: full CV, GMC certificate, DBS certificate, indemnity confirmation, two consultant referees, and a completed privileges application specifying exactly what procedures you are credentialed to perform.

  • HCA UK (The Harley Street Clinic, The Lister, The Wellington, The Portland): 6–8 weeks, highly structured process, online portal
  • Nuffield Health (Cromwell, Woking, Brighton): 4–6 weeks
  • Spire Healthcare (Spire London East, St Anthony's, Spire Bushey): 4–6 weeks
  • UCLH Private: 6–8 weeks; requires separate application to UCLH's private healthcare arm
  • The London Clinic / King Edward VII's: 6–10 weeks; more bespoke
💡 Apply to at least two hospitals simultaneously. Credentialing at one does not automatically transfer to another. Many consultants begin credentialing well before their Trust notification to avoid delays.

Step 1 — Insurance Verification

Before booking a PMI patient, verify active cover. You or your secretary should confirm:

  • Policy is active and not lapsed
  • Planned consultation / treatment is covered under their specific plan (not all plans cover cancer treatment)
  • Whether a GP referral letter is required (most insurers still mandate this)
  • The patient's excess amount — they pay this directly; invoice accordingly
  • Your Healthcode Billing Reference is on the insurer's recognised consultant list

Step 2 — Pre-Authorisation

Many treatments require pre-auth before you start. Without a valid auth code, the insurer can legitimately decline the claim entirely.

✅ Usually NOT required for:

  • Initial consultations (20300)
  • Standard follow-ups (20310)
  • Video consultations (20400)
  • Second opinion letters (20500)

🔴 ALWAYS required for:

  • All chemotherapy (X70xx)
  • Immunotherapy / biologics
  • All complex treatment planning
  • All invasive procedures
  • Infusions (bisphosphonates, etc.)
⚠️ Record the authorisation code on every invoice line item. Bupa and AXA have become significantly stricter since 2024. A missing code will delay payment by 4–6 weeks minimum.

Steps 4–5 — Generating & Submitting the Invoice

Submit within 7 days of service (most insurers have claim submission windows; some are 3 months, but earlier is always better). A correctly formed invoice must include:

  • Your Healthcode Billing Reference
  • Patient name, date of birth, policy number, and membership number
  • Date of service and treating site
  • CCSD code(s) with pre-auth reference where applicable
  • ICD-10 diagnosis code (increasingly mandatory for all major insurers)
  • Your bank details (set once in Healthcode; applied automatically)
✅ Payment timeline: Bupa and AXA typically pay within 28–30 days of a correctly submitted invoice. Smaller insurers 45–60 days. Build a simple reconciliation tracker in Xero or a spreadsheet.

Self-Pay Strategy

Self-pay patients pay you directly. No pre-auth, no insurer fee schedule, no claim submissions — and the highest margin.

✅ Advantages

  • No pre-authorisation at all
  • You set the rates (publish on PHIN)
  • Faster payment — upfront or same-day card charge
  • No claim submissions or reconciliation delays
  • No insurer recognition requirements

⚠️ Considerations

  • Fees must be published on PHIN (legal)
  • Drug costs still billed by hospital — clarify upfront
  • Cancellation / DNA policy must be in writing at booking
  • Patients may be unaware of total cost trajectory
  • Some patients will ask if you can "help" with insurance — you cannot

Option A: Sole Trader

You are the business. All profits taxed as personal income via Self Assessment.

  • Zero setup cost; total privacy of accounts
  • 40–45% tax on income above the higher-rate threshold
  • Personal liability for any business debts
  • Simple: just register on gov.uk for Self Assessment
Best if annual private profit < £40k

Option B: Limited Company

You are a director and shareholder. More complex but significantly more tax-efficient at higher incomes.

  • 19–25% Corporation Tax (vs 40%+ income tax)
  • Draw salary (below NI threshold) + dividends structure
  • Employ a spouse/partner if they genuinely contribute to the business
  • Public filing at Companies House; accountancy fees £2,000–4,000/yr
Best if annual private profit > £50k
📋

HMRC Checklist

  • Register for Self Assessment as soon as private income exceeds £1,000. Do not wait until year end.
  • Making Tax Digital (MTD) is mandatory. Use Xero, FreeAgent, or QuickBooks — all MTD-compliant and integrate with Healthcode.
  • VAT: Medical care is VAT-exempt. Medicolegal reports and expert witness work are not exempt. If non-exempt income exceeds £90,000, you must register for VAT.
  • Set aside 35% of every invoice into a separate savings account from day one. Payment on Account can catch consultants off guard in year two.
  • Submit by 31 January for the preceding tax year. Payments on Account may also be due in July.
  • NHS Pension: Private income does not contribute to your NHS pension. Consider a SIPP for private income — take advice from a specialist medical accountant.

Virtual Medical Secretary

Do not hire a full-time secretary at the outset. A virtual service answers calls under your name, books appointments, handles pre-auth calls, and chases invoices.

  • Medserv — pay-per-minute; oncology-experienced teams
  • The Medical Secretary — monthly retainer; fully managed
  • In-house allocated PA — some hospitals offer this for sessional consultants
~£500–£1,500/month depending on volume

AI Clinical Scribe

Record the consultation on your phone; AI generates a structured GP letter in under 30 seconds. Eliminates audio typing and most dictation costs.

  • Heidi Health — UK GDPR-compliant; widely adopted
  • Freed — US-origin but used in UK private practice
  • Ambio — UK-first, emerging option

Always review AI output before sending — errors do occur, particularly with drug names and dosing.

~£100–£200/month

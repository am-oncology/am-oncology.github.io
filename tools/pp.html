The UK Private Medical
Practice Blueprint

A comprehensive operational and strategic roadmap for NHS consultants entering independent practice — covering regulatory, financial, technological, and clinical governance requirements.

All Specialties Applicable across UK consultant grades

8 Core Phases From NHS contract review to Year 1 income

Transitioning from a purely institutional NHS framework into independent private practice represents one of the most consequential professional decisions a consultant will make. You are no longer only a clinician — you become simultaneously an elite clinical authority and an independent corporate entity operating in a complex regulated marketplace. The financial rewards can be substantial, but only for those who approach the transition with disciplined operational planning.

This blueprint outlines the exact sequential onboarding pathways, structural safeguards, and technology assessments required to build an efficient, fully compliant, and legally optimised private practice within the United Kingdom. It is written from the perspective of a clinical oncologist building a London-based practice, but the regulatory, financial, and operational principles apply uniformly across all consultant specialties.

01
Phase One

Legal, Regulatory & Financial Safeguards

Before engaging with any private medical facility or admitting your first patient, your business foundation and personal legal protections must be firmly in place. These are non-negotiable prerequisites, not administrative formalities. Skipping or deferring any of these steps exposes you to regulatory censure, personal financial liability, and potential GMC fitness-to-practise proceedings.

Step 1.1
The NHS Contract & The 11-PA Clause

Under the standard 2003 NHS Consultant Contract, your private practice intentions must be formally declared to your clinical director and trust management before commencing any activity. If you hold a standard 10-Programmed Activity schedule, your trust retains the right of first refusal to offer you an 11th clinical PA. If formally offered and refused — for the purpose of conducting private work — you risk losing incremental salary progression under the contract's additional PA provisions.

All private clinics, operating lists, and administrative windows must be conducted strictly outside your contracted NHS hours, outside any flexible working arrangements, and never during active on-call responsibilities. Many trusts require a formal declaration of outside interests form on an annual basis, and this must be updated to reflect your private practice once established.

Step 1.2
Commercial Medical Indemnity

The Crown Indemnity framework — which covers you for clinical negligence within your NHS employment — provides precisely zero coverage for private medical practice. This is a frequently misunderstood gap that carries catastrophic financial consequences if not addressed.

You must either upgrade your membership tier with a traditional Medical Defence Organisation (MDU, MPS, or MDDUS) or transition to a specialist commercial Lloyd's-backed indemnity insurer. Your premium is calculated against your specialty risk category and your projected gross private turnover. For new entrants, premiums can be minimised by declaring a restricted introductory turnover band, which is then adjusted upward on annual renewal as your practice volume grows.

  • Coverage floor: Practising privileges at major hospital groups require a minimum indemnity cover of £10 million per claim
  • Claims-made vs. occurrence: Understand which policy type you hold — claims-made policies require a tail (run-off) policy when you cease practice
  • Cyber liability: As a sole data controller, consider adding a cyber liability rider to your policy, as patient data breaches carry separate ICO penalties
Step 1.3
ICO Data Governance Registration

Operating as an independent private practitioner means you are legally classified as a separate and sole Data Controller under UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018. You are handling Special Category Data — patient medical histories, diagnoses, prescriptions, and financial information — which carries the highest tier of regulatory obligation.

You are legally mandated to register with the Information Commissioner's Office and pay the statutory data protection fee. For solo consultants or micro-businesses, this falls under Tier 1 (£47/year via direct debit). You are also required to maintain a Record of Processing Activities (RoPA), have a lawful basis for each type of processing, publish a compliant privacy notice, and document your data retention and disposal policies. If you use any cloud-based EPR or messaging system with servers outside the UK, you must conduct a Transfer Impact Assessment.

Step 1.4
Business Structure: Sole Trader vs. Limited Company

Most consultants begin as sole traders — the simplest structure, requiring only self-assessment registration with HMRC. However, once your private practice generates consistent profits above approximately £50,000–£60,000 per year, a limited company structure can become significantly more tax-efficient, allowing you to pay yourself a combination of salary and dividends and to control the timing of your personal income.

IR35 Awareness

If you operate through a limited company and provide services to a private hospital that controls your working pattern, IR35 "off-payroll" rules may apply. HMRC scrutinises medical service companies closely. Take specialist tax advice before incorporating.

Regardless of structure, establishing a dedicated, isolated business bank account from day one is essential. All patient payments, insurer remittances, and professional fees must flow exclusively through this account. All business outgoings — indemnity, software subscriptions, secretarial costs — must leave from it. This structural isolation is not optional best practice; it is foundational to transparent bookkeeping and minimises accounting costs at year-end.

Step 1.5
CQC Registration Considerations

The Care Quality Commission regulates healthcare providers in England. If you intend to provide regulated activities — which includes most clinical consultations, investigations, and treatments — the registration requirements depend critically on your working model.

  • Practising at an existing registered provider (e.g., Spire, Nuffield, HCA): You do not require your own CQC registration — the hospital's registration covers the regulated activity
  • Operating your own consulting rooms (e.g., a leased clinic space, rooms in a medical centre not already registered): You must register directly with the CQC as a provider of regulated activities
  • Telemedicine / remote consultations only: Advice of a specialist health regulatory solicitor is recommended, as the position is nuanced and evolving

Phase Two

Practice Infrastructure & Technology

A modern private practice operates on digital workflow automation. Every unnecessary administrative burden consumes time that could either be clinical or simply your own. The goal is to achieve a lean overhead model with fixed costs proportional to your actual activity level.

Electronic Patient Record (EPR) — Comparative Assessment

Your EPR is the operational nucleus of your daily practice. It must provide cloud accessibility for remote support teams, native integration with the Healthcode clearinghouse, electronic prescribing with FP10 private prescription support, and GDPR-compliant data architecture. The Calendar Desynchronisation Risk

Premium practice management platforms offer public-facing online booking forms for patients, but these do not automatically synchronise with the internal room booking grids of private hospital providers (Nuffield Health, Spire, HCA, etc.). Your administrative team must cross-check and manually align hospital room allocations with your digital calendar at every booking cycle to avoid costly double-booking errors and the reputational damage of cancelled patient appointments.

Healthcode: The Clearinghouse Architecture

Invoicing private medical insurers is not a matter of sending a PDF attachment. Transmitting unencrypted patient claims data via standard email is a UK GDPR violation. All claims to insurance providers — Bupa, AXA Health, Aviva, Vitality, WPA, Cigna — must route through Healthcode, the encrypted, standards-compliant digital clearance hub for the entire UK private medical industry. Your chosen EPR must hold a live, tested Healthcode integration before you see your first insured patient.

Administrative Support Model

The temptation to employ a fixed-salary medical secretary from day one is a common and costly early mistake. Until your practice volume is reliably generating 15–20+ consultations per week, a fixed employee creates a structural overhead that persists during slow periods and introduces employment law obligations. The operationally optimal model for a new practice is a specialist pay-as-you-go medical secretarial agency or a dedicated billing service.

03
Phase Three

Accreditation, Governance & Launch

The final launch phase aligns your clinical credentials with private hospital providers and insurance networks. The processes involved are not rapid — allow three to six months for practising privileges and PMI recognition to be fully approved.

Step 3.1
Securing Practising Privileges

Private consultants do not lease clinical space in the conventional commercial sense. You apply for Practising Privileges at your chosen independent hospital network, which grants you an honorary contract to use their facilities, outpatient rooms, theatres, and inpatient wards. Applications are reviewed by the hospital's Medical Advisory Committee (MAC) and typically require:

  • Current full GMC registration with Specialist Register entry in your clinical specialty
  • Substantive NHS consultant contract — locum or fixed-term contracts may not satisfy all hospitals' requirements
  • Enhanced DBS certificate (within 3 years) or subscription to the DBS Update Service
  • Professional indemnity certificate evidencing cover of at least £10 million per claim
  • Two professional references, typically from your clinical director and a senior colleague
  • Evidence of current appraisal and revalidation status — a Responsible Officer letter is often required
Step 3.2
Clinical Governance & MDT Integration

You cannot operate your private practice in a clinical vacuum, and the regulatory framework makes no allowance for it. Any high-risk treatment protocols, complex surgical interventions, or advanced therapies must follow peer-reviewed guidelines and be subject to structured oversight.

You must formally align with either the private hospital's internal Multidisciplinary Team or secure a documented reciprocal agreement allowing your NHS trust's MDT to cross-govern and review your private cases. Every element of private activity — clinical outcomes, adverse incident metrics, audits, and patient complaints — must be declared and reviewed at your annual NHS appraisal. Failure to do so constitutes a serious governance breach and can trigger a Responsible Officer referral to the GMC.

Step 3.3
Private Medical Insurer (PMI) Recognition

Recognition as an approved specialist with each major insurer is a separate application process from practising privileges and must be pursued in parallel. Most insurers require newer consultants to accept a "fee-assured" model — you accept their pre-set fee schedules as full payment and agree not to apply any patient top-up billing.

Recognition timescales vary considerably: Bupa and AXA Health typically process applications within 8–12 weeks, while smaller providers can be faster. The major UK PMI providers are:

Tier 1 Providers Bupa · AXA Health · Aviva
Tier 2 Providers Vitality · WPA · Cigna · Healix · BUPA Global
Top-Up Billing Prohibition

Under Bupa's recognition terms, billing an insured patient for any amount above the agreed fee schedule — including so-called "shortfall" charges — results in immediate de-recognition. The Competition and Markets Authority (CMA) has investigated consultant billing practices. Self-pay patients are not subject to these restrictions, but all charges must be transparently communicated in writing in advance of treatment.

Phase Four

Taxation Mechanics & Financial Compliance

Because your NHS consultant salary already places you in the higher or additional rate bracket, virtually every pound of private practice profit will be taxed at your top marginal rate. Without active financial planning, a significant portion of gross revenue will be consumed by deferred tax liabilities that accumulate silently during your first operating year.

The £100,000 Personal Allowance Trap

If your total income (NHS salary + private profits) exceeds £100,000, HMRC withdraws your £12,570 personal allowance at a rate of £1 for every £2 earned above the threshold. This creates an effective marginal tax rate of approximately 60% on income between £100,000 and £125,140. Pension contributions made during this income band are consequently one of the most tax-efficient moves available to a consultant.

The Payment on Account Cash-Flow Trap

When your self-assessed tax liability first exceeds £1,000, HMRC requires Payments on Account — advance payments toward your anticipated next-year tax bill. In your first self-assessment January, you face a double payment: 100% of the current year's tax bill, plus 50% in advance for the following year. Without an established reserve, this can generate a severe cash-flow crisis.

The 80% Protective Shield Rule

If 80% or more of your total tax liability is collected at source through your NHS PAYE payroll, you are completely exempt from Payments on Account. Your annual private tax obligation settles as a single January balancing payment, without advance requirements — provided your private earnings remain relatively modest against your NHS salary. As your private income grows, this exemption may cease to apply, making a mid-year tax review with your accountant essential.

05
Phase Five

Fee Setting & Income Modelling

Setting fees correctly is one of the most consequential decisions in your first year. Underpricing erodes professional positioning and creates a ceiling you will find difficult to raise. Overpricing without the referral volume to justify it will leave your clinic lists empty. A dual framework — one for insured patients, one for self-pay — governs the practical reality.

The Insured vs. Self-Pay Distinction

For insured patients, your fee is not your decision — it is the PMI's. The major insurers maintain internal fee schedules (often referenced to the British United Provident Association schedule or their own proprietary rate cards). Your obligation under recognition agreements is to accept these as full settlement. The self-pay market, by contrast, gives you full pricing autonomy, and this is where thoughtful fee architecture matters most.

Fee Transparency Obligation

The Private Healthcare Information Network (PHIN) requires consultants and providers to publish fee information. The CMA also mandated improved price transparency following its 2014 market investigation. You must provide self-pay patients with a clear written breakdown of anticipated costs — including facility fees, anaesthetic fees, and any likely follow-up charges — before obtaining consent to treat. Failure to do so is increasingly a source of complaints and medicolegal risk.

Phase Six

Building Your Referral Network

The clinical quality of your private practice is necessary but not sufficient. Your income trajectory is ultimately determined by the volume and quality of your referral pipeline. This requires deliberate, sustained relationship-building across GP networks, allied consultant colleagues, and the private hospital ecosystem.

GP Engagement Strategy

General practitioners are the gatekeepers of the largest referral volume in the UK private system. The most effective GP engagement strategies combine professional visibility with genuine clinical utility:

Clinic Open Days

Host brief, CME-accredited educational sessions at your private hospital facility, ideally at lunchtime. These should cover clinical updates in your specialty rather than promotional content about your practice. GPs who attend and respect your expertise become natural referrers.

Rapid Turnaround Letters

The single most effective referral retention tool is a high-quality, same-day or next-day consultation letter. GPs who receive your letters within 24 hours — with a clear management plan and explicit follow-up communication — will default to sending you their complex cases.

Accessibility & Responsiveness

Make it simple for GPs to reach you directly for advice on whether a patient needs a private referral. A direct email address for clinical queries — answered within one business day — is a powerful differentiator from NHS pathways and builds significant goodwill.

Directory Listings & Digital Presence

Ensure your profile is complete and accurate on the major private referral platforms: Doctify, Top Doctors, and your hospital provider's consultant directory. Patient-facing reviews on these platforms are now a significant driver of self-pay referrals. Claim and verify your NHS Choices profile as it ranks prominently in Google searches.

Phase Seven

Common Pitfalls & How to Avoid Them

The following represent the most frequently encountered operational, financial, and medicolegal errors made by consultants in their first two years of independent practice. Forewarned is forearmed.

Pitfall Why It Happens Mitigation
Delayed invoicing Busy clinical weeks cause invoices to lag weeks or months behind consultations — debtors age, insurers apply time limits Invoice within 24–48 hours of every consultation. Commission billing service if volume is a problem
No signed consent to charges Self-pay patients dispute invoices if they were not explicitly informed of fees in advance Obtain signed financial consent before every self-pay appointment. Document clearly in EPR
Mixing NHS and private clinical records Temptation to use NHS infrastructure for private documentation to save time Maintain entirely separate clinical records in your private EPR. Using NHS systems for private work is a serious governance breach
Underestimating tax reserves Private income feels like "extra money" and is spent rather than ring-fenced Reserve 45% of every private payment into a separate account immediately on receipt. Treat it as already spent
Not declaring at NHS appraisal Confusion about what needs declaring, or wish to avoid awkward conversations Declare all private income, complaints, adverse events, and medicolegal work annually. Non-disclosure is a GMC fitness-to-practise matter
Treating insured patients as self-pay Administrative error or patient confusion about their policy status Verify insurance authorisation — including specific authorisation code and approved number of sessions — before every first appointment
No chasing debtors policy Clinical culture makes billing follow-up feel uncomfortable Establish a clear written policy: automated reminder at 14 days, formal letter at 30, referral to collection service at 60
Premature practice expansion Enthusiasm leads to hiring staff, leasing consulting rooms, or opening multiple sites before revenue is stable Maintain a lean overhead model for at least 18–24 months. Scale expenses in response to proven revenue, not projected revenue
